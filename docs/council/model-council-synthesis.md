## Comprehensive Analysis

The three models converged with unusual force on the headline call: **pivot the product, keep the architecture.** None recommended killing it, and none endorsed shipping the spec as written. When three frontier models independently reach the same verdict from different research paths, that is the highest-confidence signal in this review — treat "pivot" as settled, and spend your energy on *which* pivot.

The strongest agreement, and the most actionable, is the **Gmail scope inversion**. All three independently found that the spec has it backwards: creating drafts requires `gmail.compose`, which Google classifies as a *restricted* scope that triggers the annual CASA security assessment, while merely *sending* via `gmail.send` is only *sensitive* and does not. Your "safe, review-free" free tier (draft-only) is in fact the tier that forces the audit. This is a factual correction, not a judgment call — fix it regardless of which direction you pivot. Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT 5.5 also both dismantled the "Composio manages consent" assumption with primary Composio docs: managed auth shares quota and shows "Composio wants to access your account" on the consent screen, so any real product must bring its own branded Google OAuth app — at which point *you* own verification and CASA, not Composio. The convergence here means the spec's entire compliance-cost model needs rebuilding before day one.

The models also agree the competitive picture is worse than the spec admits, and their combined evidence is damning. Gemini 3.1 Pro — leaning on its Google-ecosystem knowledge — supplied the sharpest data point: Gemini Live has been *free* on both Android and iOS since May 2025, with live camera and Workspace actions, and Magic Cue is an OS-level version of the same promise now expanding to third-party apps. GPT 5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8 added that ChatGPT already drafts *and sends* Gmail with confirm-before-send. So "action-first, not chat-first" is a UI line, not a moat, and the consumer buyer already has three free or bundled ways to do this. Where they differ is instructive: Gemini says the consumer market is simply lost and you should jump straight to B2B; GPT 5.5 counsels a cheaper intermediate step — prove one narrow high-frequency workflow loop first (appointment/letter → calendar event or local email draft) using *no* restricted scopes and minimal Composio, then expand; Claude Opus 4.8 lands between them, recommending iOS-first + B2B-vertical-first but with Composio deliberately abstracted behind your own swappable interface. This is a genuine strategic fork worth your judgment: GPT's path de-risks by validating retention before spending on B2B connectors and CASA; Gemini's path skips a market it considers already dead; Opus's path hedges. Notably, Gemini also found that "Scan-to-Salesforce" apps *already ship* — so even the recommended B2B destination needs competitive diligence rather than being assumed greenfield.

On timeline, the models agree the 14-day "launch + first payments" milestone is fiction, but Claude Opus 4.8 contributed the single most decisive fact: personal Google Play accounts created after November 2023 must run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 continuous days *before* they can even apply for production — making a Day-14 Android public launch physically impossible and internal testing insufficient. Combined with GPT 5.5's per-workstream slippage math (vision extraction, Composio auth debugging, and Salesforce object-mapping each 2–10 days), the honest estimate is a solo-dev iOS TestFlight closed beta with one connector in ~2–3 weeks, and a public, verified, multi-connector launch in 6–12 weeks. The lone disagreements — whether the "Jan 28 2026" date and "Android 17 AAPM" claims are precisely right — are minor: GPT couldn't verify the exact date on Google's official page and read the AAPM claim as citing Android 16 rather than 17, while Opus confirmed both with different sources. These don't change any conclusion; they're worth a quick fact-check for the spec's credibility but nothing rests on them.

**Recommendation.** Adopt the PIVOT the council unanimously endorses, sequenced to satisfy all three models at once: (1) **immediately fix the scope model** — drop `gmail.compose`, use `gmail.send` + `drive.file`, or GPT's even-safer no-Gmail-API path via native share sheet, so v1 touches no restricted scopes and needs no CASA; (2) **go iOS-first** to sidestep the Android closed-test gate and exploit the EU Siri-AI ban window Opus identified; (3) **start with GPT's narrow wedge** — one high-frequency workflow to prove a retention loop cheaply — then move toward the B2B "scan → Salesforce/QuickBooks" vertical that Opus and Gemini both call the only durable moat, but only after checking the incumbents Gemini surfaced; (4) **wrap Composio behind your own action interface** so its documented incidents, shared-quota limits, and enterprise-only EU residency don't become single points of product, security, and compliance failure; and (5) **reprice around completed workflows, not scans, and cut the lifetime tier.** The core insight the whole council shares: your platform-legal homework was above average, but you aimed a store-safe architecture at a market three well-funded incumbents already own — redirect it at the business-tool write-back long tail they ignore, and fix the compliance facts before writing code.
